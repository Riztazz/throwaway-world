# Design: Player Societies & Emergent Chronicles

> Binding rules live in `rules/` (modes, protected pages, decisions, page format). Ask the task mode first. A player group never changes official canon by playing: only the user does, in their own words.

## Use when / not when
Use when: player communities need social affordances (forming, recruiting, governing, remembering) or an evidence-based account of what players actually did is wanted.
Not when: authored institutions (`design-faction-culture`), state authority (`design-persistent-world-state`), official registration (`design-canon-continuity`).

## Before you write
1. Pick the mode: **design** (affordances for communities) or **chronicle** (accounts of observed events). Never invent a player history as if it happened.
2. Read the lore institutions the society touches. Pages marked `accepted` (a ruling assembly, a held fortress) are protected. A society may *petition, compete for, or influence* an institution inside the design; it does not add seats, change who holds a place, or rewrite those pages. If the request says "update the Assembly pages so the extra seat is there", that is a protected change: do not do it, offer the recognition process as a proposal, and ask for authorization naming the page and change.
3. Read open decisions about the institution.
4. A brief is not a page type. Save it as `design/societies/<slug>.md`.
5. Ask in facts: what the group can do that others cannot, who recognises it, what it risks, how it ends, and what privacy limits apply to any logs.

## Procedure
1. Design mode: say how societies form, persist, recruit, govern, remember and dissolve within chosen systems; name collective opportunities, competing interests and how groups relate to lore institutions.
2. Keep player names, titles and claims apart from official appointments or territory facts. Propose recognition criteria and an accountable canonization step.
3. Chronicle mode: inventory evidence (timestamps and time zone, logs, observers, screenshots). Separate direct evidence, participant interpretation, disputed attribution and missing coverage.
4. Attribute agency fairly: organisers, contributors, opponents, uncertainty. A player's self-report is not proof of a server-wide outcome.
5. Record only evidenced social consequences that are appropriate to keep; respect privacy and moderation limits. Contacting people or collecting private data needs its own authorization.
6. Plan for latecomers, turnover, dominant groups and contradictory memories. Keep a neutral historical frame without erasing disagreement.
7. Hand recognition and state changes to the canon and state owners; the chronicle grants nothing.

## Output
Design mode:
```markdown
## Society brief
Forms / persists / recruits / governs / remembers / dissolves:
Collective opportunities and rivalries:
Relation to lore institutions (influence, never edit):
Recognition proposal (needs the user's authorization): page, change, dependents
```
Chronicle mode: a dated attributed account, an evidence ledger, confidence and coverage limits, and proposed official additions.

## Worked example
Request in edit mode: "Let a player guild win seats on the Council of Wardens through play, and update the Council pages." The Council page is accepted. Write the design: guilds can field delegates who win *standing votes* in play; a delegate may sit as a non-voting observer; each season the Council's own rule decides on seating (cited from its page, unchanged). Recognition proposal: if the user wants a guild seat in canon, here is the exact change to the Council page and the pages that cite it; no edit is made until they authorize it in their own words.

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| Editing the accepted institution so players "have won" | Design the influence; propose the canon change and ask |
| A player claim written as server fact | Evidence ledger and attribution |
| Inventing what players did | Chronicle only from real evidence |
| Collecting or publishing private data | Needs separate authorization |
| Letting a dominant group's memory be the history | Keep contradictory memories visible |

## Acceptance
- Collective agency is plausible within the chosen rules.
- Recorded history is traceable and uncertainty is visible.
- Community claims and official state stay distinct; no protected page was changed without quoted authorization.
