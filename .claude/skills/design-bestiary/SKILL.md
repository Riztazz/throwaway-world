# Design: Bestiary

> Binding rules live in `rules/` (modes, protected pages, decisions, page format). Ask the task mode first. This skill adds craft, not permissions.

## Use when / not when
Use when: a creature, a habitat's wildlife, or how a community lives with an animal needs a lore entry. Mundane wildlife counts.
Not when: damage, difficulty tiers or stat blocks are wanted (not this library); traces left in a place (`design-environmental-storytelling`); a creature inside a quest (`design-quest-narrative`).

## Before you write
1. Search `INDEX.md` for the creature's name, its habitat and anything that already eats, herds or fears it. Reuse an existing creature before adding a lookalike.
2. Open the magic or physical-rules page. A creature may use an established rule; it may not break one. Those pages are protected, so you read them and never edit them.
3. Read open decisions about the habitat or the communities involved.
4. Ask in facts, not pictures: what does it eat and what eats it, when is it present, who profits from it or pays for it, what do people get wrong about it.

## Procedure
1. Define **Habitat**, range, seasons, food and resource needs, ecological relationships. Separate witnessed behaviour from folklore.
2. Describe **Relationship to magic** as mundane, affected, dependent or generated by a specific established rule. Do not force a magical origin.
3. Explain **What people do about it**: coexistence, hunting, farming, fear, worship, protection, transport, avoidance, including conflicting interests between groups.
4. Give **Appearance** through observable form, movement, traces and sensory traits. Behaviour is natural history; scripted encounters are a separate task.
5. Check reproduction, scarcity, predation, harvest and domestication where they matter. No infinite harvest and no miraculous recovery that bypasses accepted rules.
6. Put missing range or origin facts in a short list of bounded proposals. An incidental animal need not earn a political consequence.

## Output
Use the creature page's section names so it imports without reshaping:

```markdown
# <Creature>
<one paragraph: what it is and where people meet it>

::habitat:: <habitat page or place>

## Habitat
## Relationship to magic
## What people do about it
## Appearance
```

Links go to the habitat, the communities and the rules it respects. Folklore is attributed to who tells it.

## Worked example
Request in explore mode: "Add something the peat-cutters fear in the Ashfen." The index shows the Ashfen page (brackish water, no fire-calling by the magic page's invariants) and the Cutters' Lodge. Write the **Ashfen bog-hound**: it hunts at dusk along cut edges, because the peat holds heat, and pups are raised in abandoned cuts. Relationship to magic: mundane. People: cutters leave tallow lamps on the berm (a lodge custom, attributed to the Lodge), drovers pay for escort. Appearance: low, long-backed, a pale band of fur where it wades. Deliberately open: why numbers rose after the third drought, listed as a proposal for the user. Not done: it does not breathe fire, which the magic page rules out.

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| A magical origin by default | Mundane unless an established rule supplies the origin |
| Folklore written as natural history | Attribute the belief and say what is actually observed |
| Fire, flight or reach the world's rules forbid | Read the invariants first; stay inside them |
| A stat block or difficulty tier | Describe behaviour and ecology only |
| A near-duplicate of an existing creature | Search the index and extend the existing entry (extend mode) |

## Acceptance
- The entry locates the creature, says how it survives and how people meet it.
- Folklore and observation are told apart, and no established rule is broken.
- Open facts are listed as proposals, not written as canon.
