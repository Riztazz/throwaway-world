# Design: Culture & Everyday Life

> Binding rules live in `rules/` (modes, protected pages, decisions, page format). Ask the task mode first. This skill adds craft, not permissions.

## Use when / not when
Use when: a community's daily life, customs, class and generation differences, migration or material life need writing down.
Not when: an institution's goals and power (`design-faction-culture`), beliefs and rituals (`design-cosmology-belief`), names (`design-language-naming`).

## Before you write
1. Search `INDEX.md` for the community, its region and the faction pages that name it. Open the geography, economy and history pages that constrain how people live. Economy and history pages are protected: read them, never edit them here.
2. Read open decisions about the community or its institutions.
3. A culture has no page type of its own. If the community is a faction or a region, its `## Culture` section is the home: add it in a new page, or in a non-protected page you are allowed to change in this mode. Otherwise keep the entry as a draft at `design/culture/<slug>.md`.
4. Ask in facts and consequences: who counts as a member and who does not, what do they do on an ordinary day, what do they pay for, what do outsiders misread, who disagrees.

## Procedure
1. Say who is included and how membership is understood; overlapping identities are normal. Bound the community rather than inventing every culture in the world.
2. Choose everyday details that change a scene: food and work, households and care, learning, leisure, etiquette, art, mourning, exchange, justice.
3. Tie practice to resources, infrastructure, migration, contact, pressure and memory. Geography limits responses; it does not make a people warlike, mystical or industrious.
4. Show variation by class, generation, trade, locality and dissent. Do not make each subgroup differ by one arbitrary habit.
5. Keep self-understanding, outsiders' stereotypes and observed practice apart. Mark contested accounts and the author's private facts.
6. Give at least one ordinary consequence and one pressure for change: who benefits, who pays, how a rule is negotiated, what an outsider gets wrong.
7. For real-world inspirations use evidence and transformation. Do not dress a stereotype in invented names, and do not claim cultural approval you did not get.

## Output
```markdown
## Culture            (or the draft's title)
Scope and membership: <who, who is outside, who overlaps>
Material life: <work, food, housing, what is scarce>
Practices: <3-5, each with its reason>
Variation and dissent: <class, generation, trade, place>
Causes: <contact, migration, history, resources, with page links>
What an outsider misreads: <one or two>
Proposed facts (not canon): <list>
```

## Worked example
Request in explore mode: "Everyday life for the reed-cutters of Lowmere." The index shows the Lowmere geography (tidal, brackish) and the reed trade in the economy page. Write membership (cutters, binders, and the carters who are not members), then material life (cutting in the dark half of the month because the reeds are brittle in sun, bundles priced by the tide table), then three practices with reasons, variation (binders look down on cutters; the young leave for the quay), a pressure (the new sluice is lowering the beds), and one outsider misreading (they are not "marsh-silent"; they speak low to avoid frightening the heron flocks they depend on). Proposed: a lodge name. The economy page is untouched.

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| One trait per culture (the warlike desert people) | Variation by class, generation and trade |
| Terrain as destiny | Tie practice to resources, contact and pressure |
| A checklist of customs with no reasons | Each practice has a cause and a cost |
| Stereotype with invented names | Evidence, transformation, and say what was not reviewed |
| Editing the economy or history page to explain a custom | Propose the fact and ask |

## Acceptance
- Residents have more than one plausible life and some disagreement.
- Concrete practices shape action; identity is not reducible to terrain, biology, a single faction or a borrowed stereotype.
- No protected page was changed, and proposed facts are labelled.
