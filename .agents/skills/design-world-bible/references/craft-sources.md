# Craft sources and an original style starting brief

Optional further reading for specific techniques. These sources inform craft/engineering questions; the family’s canon layers, proposal handling and MMO state tables are project authoring contracts, not frameworks validated by every linked source. A public abstract is not a watched talk, and an interface tutorial is not proof of persistent-world support.

## Selective exposition and historical depth

- [Writing Excuses: story, not an encyclopedia](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-10-20-how-do-i-write-a-story-not-an-encyclopedia/): useful for choosing information around story needs rather than exhausting world knowledge.
- [Steven Erikson interview, SFFWorld](https://www.sffworld.com/2006/01/scifiint_165/): an author perspective on writing and audience; use as craft context, not a template for exact voice.
- [Erikson's scene-annotation mirror](https://www.17thshard.com/applications/core/interface/file/attachment.php?id=19958), corroborated by the [author blog record](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/16496344-deconstructing-the-siege-of-pale-aftermath-scene): a worked source for analyzing viewpoint/exposition choices. Do not treat access to an annotation as full-series analysis or imitate signature prose.
- [Henry Jenkins: game design as narrative architecture](https://web.mit.edu/~21fms/People/henry3/games%26narrative.html): spatial evidence and player exploration; useful for environmental traces, not a camera prescription.

These are techniques to test against the task, not a universal “never explain” rule. A reader may infer history while still needing a plain immediate instruction.

## An original starting style brief

This is an original configurable brief, not an Erikson voice imitation:

- **Viewpoint:** close enough to follow the body's task and selective attention. Narrator knowledge does not automatically exceed the chosen viewpoint.
- **Detail selection:** choose what matters to work, safety, status, hunger, memory or obligation. Two telling details can do more than a catalog.
- **Unfamiliar concepts:** establish a practical consequence or familiar relation, then name the term when needed.
- **Historical depth:** use repairs, reused materials, inherited rules, altered routes and contested accounts to make several pasts coexist in the present.
- **Immediate clarity:** make location, actor and next action understandable. Reserve uncertainty for causes, motives and larger meanings when intended.
- **Density and rhythm:** vary information load. Let a dense discovery breathe in action, response or a simpler sentence; not every paragraph needs layers.
- **Language:** concrete verbs and original metaphor domains rooted in viewpoint experience. No source catchphrases, ornamental name piles or copied syntax patterns.
- **Truth:** a character's account can be biased; make attribution clear enough not to mistake it for settled author fact.

### Short original demonstration

> Mara stopped at the ferry rail. The lower bolts were new; above them, three empty holes marked where a second rail had stood before the floods. She had to lift her grain sack over the repair, and the seam spilled a pale line onto the wet boards.
>
> “Wait for the bell,” the keeper said. “Two rings means the downstream boat. One means stay here.”
>
> The copper plaque called the flood a royal rescue. Mara's grandmother called it the winter the mills were opened. Neither name made the sack lighter.

The passage orients task and action, layers material history and competing memory, and leaves a cause disputed. It is illustrative proposed fiction, not world canon or a claim about any reference work.

## Persistent serial narrative

- [Guild Wars 2: Colin Johanson on the months ahead](https://www.guildwars2.com/en/news/colin-johanson-on-guild-wars-2-in-the-months-ahead/): a primary example of thinking about an evolving online world. Its implementation choices are not required by this project.
- [Guild Wars 2: Living World Season 1 return](https://www.guildwars2.com/en/news/living-world-season-1-return-battle-for-lions-arch-is-now-live/): a concrete legacy-access example. Transfer the missed-history problem, not its use of instances.
- [GDC: The Playable Series](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034860/Game-Narrative-Summit-The-Playable): public talk abstract about seasonal narrative. Cite only the accessible abstract unless the talk itself was watched.

Shared physical state, personal knowledge and contribution credit need an explicit project/runtime contract. None of the links above proves this project's concurrency, authority or migration behavior.

## Interactive writing and review

- [ink tutorial](https://www.inklestudios.com/ink/web-tutorial/): branching/conditional authoring concepts. It is not an MMO persistence solution or a reason to choose ink without a project decision.
- [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/): select relevant communication/delivery checks; guideline inspection is not assistive-technology or player testing.
- [Writing the Other resources](https://writingtheother.com/resources/): avenues for researching representation and reviewing perspective. Listing them does not count as a lived-experience review.
- [Effective context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) and [building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents): selective retrieval and restrained orchestration. These do not require all roles to become skills or every skill to launch an agent.

For any new work-specific analysis, inspect actual accessible versions, record locators/coverage and attribute disagreements. Do not cite this reading list as evidence for facts about an unread novel or unwatched adaptation.
