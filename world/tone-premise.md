# Test World premise

## Premise

Łódź stands on the river Żółwia, and its present rules make sense only to someone who knows which older rule each one replaced. At every gate a clerk applies the posted rule. Somewhere in the city a keeper (a guild, a shrine, a remnant) still holds the rule it replaced, and some loads move only under that older rule. The recurring pressure is a load that must be delivered under a rule whose reason nobody states aloud.

::city:: Łódź
::river:: Żółwia
::first-part-scale:: one city and what can be seen in an hour's walk

The first part is one stretch of the river, about an hour's walk end to end, and its two banks. The rest of the city exists as named gates on its edge that do not open yet.

Fantasy stays in the background. It shows as a price, a refusal or a form to fill in, never as an event or a spectacle.

Unknown: why the rules were replaced. The premise needs the replacements, not their cause.
Unknown: whether any keeper's account of the past is true.
Unknown: how old the city is and how its years are counted.
Unknown: what the fantasy in the background is.

## Player Fantasy / Reader Experience

In the game the player is one novice porter among many. The satisfaction is reading a gate that could not be read last week: a fee paid, a reading bought, a licence from one keeper that closes a door at another. Progression is access, not power. Nobody in the world treats the player as special, and the player's choice of which book to carry under helps decide which keepers stand and which fold.

In the stories the reader follows one character's knowledge only, in close third person and past tense. A fact reaches the reader after the character has paid for it. The ending settles the practical problem (the load is delivered, refused or lost) and leaves the cost of the rule open.

## Tone Guardrails

- Humour: dry, from procedure, such as a stamp of the wrong colour or a dodge. Never puns, and never jokes that need the reader to know the setting's lore.
- Dread: stated as a logistical fact, then the next step. Never exclamations or dramatic build-up.
- Violence: rare, told as a fact and its consequence. Never spectacle, gore or a fight as a set piece.
- Intimacy: favours owed, and trust given to a clerk or a porter. Nothing sexual (PEGI 16).
- Ambiguity: `Unknown:` lines and attributed accounts ("the porters' book holds..."). Never a narrator or a character who turns out to be right about the past.
- Fantasy: a line item, such as a price, a refusal or a form. Never a marvel described for its own sake.
- Scale: a reach, a bridge, a stair. Never a war, a god or a city-wide event.

No single mood is required in every scene. A scene may be funny, tense or flat, and the plainest sentence is the gravest.

## Design Pillars

- Every rule has a predecessor | test: a rule entry gives the rule today, the rule it replaced, why it changed, and who still keeps the old one | counterexample: a rule that simply exists
- Facts are paid for | test: the character or player pays a fee, time or a favour before learning a reason | counterexample: a clerk who explains the history unprompted
- The past is held by people | test: every account of why is attributed to someone, and where the world does not say it reads `Unknown:` | counterexample: the narrator states the true reason
- Consequences are practical | test: the scene ends with a load delivered, refused or lost, and the cost of the rule left open | counterexample: the scene ends with a revelation of who really runs the city
- Nobody is special | test: an NPC treats the player as one of many porters, on the same terms as anyone | counterexample: someone recognises the player as the one
- The crowd decides which pasts survive | test: a keeper's standing traces to what players carried, and a fold is posted as a notice | counterexample: a keeper falls by plot decree
- The marvel is a line item | test: anything fantastic appears as a price, a refusal or a form | counterexample: a marvel described as spectacle

These pillars are themes. This page states no physical law of the world.
