The lower quay ran from the Salt Stair to the tannery wall, a hundred and ten paces by a porter's count, and at first light it belonged to the barges. They lay two deep along the stone with their ropes crossed, and the ropes, wet from the night, had darkened the cobbles to the colour of strong tea. The barrels on the nearest deck stood in rows as straight as the lines of a ledger. The air smelled of brine and of the tannery, in that order, with the tannery winning once the sun cleared the roofs. A chain ran from the ferry post to the far bank, slack, dripping onto the water one drop at a time. Nobody on the quay spoke much. The weigh-house bell on the upper stair had not yet rung, and until it did, nothing that was carried would be paid for.
