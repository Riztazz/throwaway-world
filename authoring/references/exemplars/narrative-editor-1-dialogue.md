purpose: Shows an edit at line level: which tests fail, what the edit keeps, and what it drops and flags for the writer. The edit serves the effect, not any author's voice. Illustrative and not canon.

## Draft

"Is the south gate open?" Marek asked anxiously.

"No," said the clerk angrily, "it is closed because of the inspection, which was ordered last week by the Office, and which nobody is happy about!"

## Tests applied

- The dodge carries the information (dialogue): fails. The clerk answers directly and then explains.
- Understatement and flat register (dialogue, tone): fails. Two adverbs name the feelings, and the exclamation mark raises the voice.
- Practical consequence: present (the gate is shut) but stated, not shown.

## Edit

"Is the south gate open?"

"Bridge toll's the same as yesterday."

"That isn't what I asked."

"Same as yesterday." The clerk turned a page that did not need turning.

## Notes for the writer

Kept: the gate is closed, and the clerk will not say so. Dropped: the inspection and the Office's order. If the inspection matters to the plot, it should reach the reader through a trace (a thread, a stamp on the gate) in a later line. Otherwise accept the cut. Not changed: the character names and the setting.
