# narrative-editor: technique cards

Status: proposed (the user approves the set by approving ANALYSIS.md and the sample pairs). Sample passages are illustrative and written for this world in English (D-0008); names and details in them are not canon and are not registered.

- Primary: Grzędowicz (hard): the tests for mood detail, comparison from the trade and dodging speech.
- Blend: + Miéville (soft): the flat-register test.
- How they combine: The editor applies each card's Test line to a draft and reports which tests fail, without rewriting toward any author's voice.
- Wins on conflict: Miéville's flat-register test on any conflict with a mood-detail test.
- Not taken: Editing a draft toward imitation of a source; the tests check effect, not phrasing.
- Atoms for this role: grzedowicz.tone.1, grzedowicz.prose.1, grzedowicz.dialogue.1, mieville.tone.1

## One material detail carries the mood

- Id: grzedowicz.tone.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: The atmosphere is felt through one concrete thing of the place and is not stated as a mood.
- Mechanism: (1) Choose one detail from the place's work or materials: tannery smell, a chain on a ferry stair. (2) Place it where the character's attention would land. (3) Let a practical consequence of the detail be what the scene turns on. (4) No mood adjectives.
- Test: Remove every mood word. Is the mood still there?
- In this world: The restraint rule caps this at one detail per scene, and the detail must cost something (a delay, a stain, a wet hand).
- Plain: It was a gloomy, cold dawn on the river and Marek felt uneasy.
- Applied: At dawn the ferry chain was still wet. It left a rust-coloured line across Marek's palm that would not wash off before the first weigh-in.
- Avoid: Piling up sensory detail; the source is described as rich and painterly, and that register is not taken (conflict A). Mood adjectives.

## Comparison from the trade

- Id: grzedowicz.prose.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: Description feels owned by the place because its comparisons come from the place's own work and materials.
- Mechanism: (1) For each comparison, pick from the character's trade or the city's materials: rope, water, ledger, brine. (2) Put it in one clause. (3) One per paragraph at most. (4) The comparison must be checkable: a length, a weight, a smell.
- Test: Could this comparison appear unchanged in a story set in any other city? If so, replace it.
- In this world: Comparisons come from carrying, weighing, the river, the tannery and the ledger.
- Plain: The old warehouse was huge and dark, like a cave.
- Applied: The warehouse ran back the length of a rope-walk, and in its dark the barrels stood shoulder to shoulder like a queue at the weigh-house.
- Avoid: Stock comparisons. Several comparisons in a row. Borrowing a source's images.

## The dodge carries the information

- Id: grzedowicz.dialogue.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: Speech shows rank, risk and knowledge through what a speaker avoids and where they steer.
- Mechanism: (1) The asker puts a plain question. (2) The answerer replies to a neighbouring question: price, place, person. (3) The omitted fact can be recovered from the shape of the dodge. (4) No tag explains the evasion.
- Test: Does the answer dodge the question and carry the information in the dodge?
- In this world: Clerks, foremen and old porters answer about tolls and hours when the real answer is about a rule.
- Plain: "Is the south gate open?" "No, it is closed because of the inspection."
- Applied: "Is the south gate open?" "The bridge toll is the same as yesterday." "That isn't what I asked." "Same as yesterday, I said."
- Avoid: Evasion that hides nothing. An explaining tag ("he said evasively"). Copying any source's exchanges.

## Flat register for large facts

- Id: mieville.tone.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: Strange or grave conditions read as credible because the narration treats them as ordinary working facts.
- Mechanism: (1) State the large fact in the same sentence length and register as a delivery note. (2) Place it between two practical details. (3) Do not comment or evaluate. (4) The consequence is a form, a delay or a changed hour.
- Test: Is the gravest sentence of the scene as short as, or plainer than, the sentences around it?
- In this world: Flood, loss and the fantastic background are all written in the register of the weigh-house board.
- Plain: Horrifyingly, the river rose over the whole lower quarter in the night and drowned several families.
- Applied: The river took the lower quarter in the night. By morning the weigh-house had moved its hours to the upper stair, and the tally of those found went up on the board beside the ferry rates.
- Avoid: Sentimental flatness that is really a joke. Emotion deleted entirely. Copying a source's noir voice.
- Evidence: The City & The City (China Miéville; edition not given), no locator; not read. Type: memory only (the web fetch failed and no search was made). Not read: the text. Confidence: low. Spoiler ceiling: premise level only.
