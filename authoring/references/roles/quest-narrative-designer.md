## Decoder at the threshold

- Id: grzedowicz.world.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: The reader learns the place at the pace, and for the price, that the newcomer pays for each fact, so the world feels lived in and not explained.
- Mechanism: (1) The viewpoint character is new to the place and has a job that forces contact with its rules. (2) A custom is met first as an obstacle: a refused load, a barred stair. (3) Someone gives a partial explanation, briefly. (4) The character acts on it and pays a small cost. (5) The full reason arrives later, or never. No local delivers a lecture.
- Test: Can you point to the line where the newcomer pays for a fact (time, coin, a wrong turn) before being given it?
- In this world: The novice porter meets each current regulation as a refusal at a gate; the older rule it replaced is found afterwards in the archive. The commission loop of the game has the same order: refusal, cost, partial answer, lookup.
- Plain: The foreman explained that porters may not cross the Salt Stair at noon. Long ago the ferry company closed it at high water. The company was gone, but the rule had stayed in the porters' book. Ewa nodded and went round the long way.
- Applied: The gate-clerk laid his stick across the stair. "Noon." Ewa had the sack on her back and the fee already counted. She went round by the tannery: forty minutes, a quarter of the fee. Later old Drozd said, not looking up, "Used to be the water. Ask the weigh-house what it is now."
- Avoid: A local who explains. A newcomer who turns out to be special; the mission-of-destiny shape (excluded). Copying any source's names, gear or scenes.

## The dodge carries the information

- Id: grzedowicz.dialogue.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: Speech shows rank, risk and knowledge through what a speaker avoids and where they steer.
- Mechanism: (1) The asker puts a plain question. (2) The answerer replies to a neighbouring question: price, place, person. (3) The omitted fact can be recovered from the shape of the dodge. (4) No tag explains the evasion.
- Test: Does the answer dodge the question and carry the information in the dodge?
- In this world: Clerks, foremen and old porters answer about tolls and hours when the real answer is about a rule.
- Plain: "Is the south gate open?" "No, it is closed because of the inspection."
- Applied: "Is the south gate open?" "The bridge toll is the same as yesterday." "That isn't what I asked." "Same as yesterday, I said."
- Avoid: Evasion that hides nothing. An explaining tag ("he said evasively"). Copying any source's exchanges.

## Understatement under pressure

- Id: grzedowicz.dialogue.2        Binding: hard
- Effect: Danger and cost are carried by dry understatement, so the scene stays factual and the reader supplies the weight.
- Mechanism: (1) A character states the hazard as a logistical fact. (2) Short sentence, no exclamation. (3) The listener answers with the next practical step. (4) Feeling shows only as behaviour: a pause, a tool put down.
- Test: Can the line be read aloud flat without losing its weight?
- In this world: Fits the tone "rzeczowy i powściągliwy". Used in emergencies on the river and at the gates.
- Plain: "The bridge is collapsing! We are all going to die!" he shouted.
- Applied: "Third span's going." He set the sack down. "Two at a time, and leave the carts."
- Avoid: Jokes that release the tension. Stoicism as a pose. Understatement in every line.

## Case against rule

- Id: mieville.rules.2        Binding: soft
- Effect: The plot moves by applying a rule to a specific case, and the reader learns the rule by watching where it fails to fit.
- Mechanism: (1) State the case: a load, a person, a document. (2) Apply the rule in a short exchange with an official. (3) The case fits the letter but not the intent, or the reverse. (4) The outcome is practical: a stamp, a refusal, a fine. (5) Next case.
- Test: Does each scene end with a ruling on a specific case?
- In this world: The shape of a commission in the game, and of a story built around one disputed load.
- Plain: "This crate is not allowed." "Why?" "Because it is not sealed."
- Applied: "Sealed?" "Sealed." The clerk turned the crate to the light. "Your seal is the new pattern, the crate is old stock. New seal on old stock is a transfer, and a transfer is the cooper's to sign."
- Avoid: Courtroom speeches. A clerk who is only an obstacle. Procedure that never produces a consequence.
