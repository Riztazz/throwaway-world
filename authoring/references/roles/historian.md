# historian: technique cards

Status: proposed (the user approves the set by approving ANALYSIS.md and the sample pairs). Sample passages are illustrative and written for this world in English (D-0008); names and details in them are not canon and are not registered.

- Primary: Grzędowicz (hard): how the past surfaces in the present.
- Blend: + Miéville (soft): a rule shown in behaviour before its history is told.
- How they combine: Miéville gives the order (habit first, reason later). Grzędowicz gives the layering (custom outliving its cause; present errand beside an old record).
- Wins on conflict: Miéville on order of reveal; Grzędowicz on how the past feels.
- Not taken: Epic chronicle, wars of gods, a single secret history that explains the city.
- Atoms for this role: grzedowicz.history.1, grzedowicz.world.3, mieville.rules.1

## Practice outlives its reason

- Id: grzedowicz.history.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: An old custom feels real because people keep it without knowing why, and the reason, when it surfaces, is smaller or stranger than the custom.
- Mechanism: (1) Show the practice done correctly and without comment. (2) Put a wrong or partial folk reason in a character's mouth. (3) Later reveal the documentary reason: a cost, an accident, a quarrel. (4) The practice continues regardless.
- Test: Does a character keep a practice while the narrative later narrows or contradicts their reason for it?
- In this world: The premise itself. Each present rule is read through the earlier rule it replaced; a habit is the trace the earlier rule left in bodies.
- Plain: Porters touch the river-post before their first load. It was set up to mark the water level for the old toll, which was abolished long ago.
- Applied: Every porter touched the river-post before the first load. Kasia asked why. "For luck." The post had been set to read the water for the toll. The toll was gone, the mark worn flat, and still the hand went out.
- Avoid: A tidy reveal that explains everything. Mysticism without a documentary layer. Using real religious practices.

## Two scales in one scene

- Id: grzedowicz.world.3        Binding: hard
- Effect: A small present errand is read against an older, larger frame set beside it, so the reader sees both the errand and what it rests on.
- Mechanism: (1) Interleave the errand with a fragment from another scale: a ledger line, a rule's text, a record. (2) Let the fragment arrive uncommented. (3) Return to the errand with one shared word or object now carrying the fragment's weight. (4) Never state the connection.
- Test: Does one word or object appear in both layers, with the connection left unstated?
- In this world: In stories, an errand plus a citation from an old rule-book. In the game, a commission text plus an archive entry the player finds afterwards.
- Plain: Ola carried the crate to the weigh-house. The rule said crates must carry a seal. The rule had been made after a fire.
- Applied: Ola set the crate down at the weigh-house. The clerk pressed the seal into the wax. [Cooper's Row ledger, year of the third burning: Crates to bear the seal, that the owner be known when the owner is ash.] The wax was still warm when she lifted it.
- Avoid: A narrator who joins the two layers for the reader. Long quoted documents. Tying every scene to an archive.

## The rule is lived before it is explained

- Id: mieville.rules.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: A prohibition feels binding because characters obey it automatically; the reader senses its weight through habit.
- Mechanism: (1) Show characters adjusting a movement or a glance without comment. (2) Give the adjustment a small cost. (3) A later scene shows what happens when someone fails to adjust. (4) Any explanation comes from a person who has reason to be brief.
- Test: Is the rule shown in someone's behaviour before any sentence states it?
- In this world: Porters' habits at certain doors, stairs and stalls, each tied to a rule that has since been replaced.
- Plain: Porters are not allowed to use the east door of the weigh-house.
- Applied: The porters came to the weigh-house in a line and at the east door each one angled a shoulder to the wall, as if the door were wet paint. Teodor, last in line, went straight at it and put his hand on the latch.
- Avoid: Making the rule the secret at the centre of the story; here it stays ordinary. Copying a source's central conceit or terms.
