# culture-builder: technique cards

Status: proposed (the user approves the set by approving ANALYSIS.md and the sample pairs). Sample passages are illustrative and written for this world in English (D-0008); names and details in them are not canon and are not registered.

- Primary: Grzędowicz (hard): societies read through a familiar institution, and customs that outlive their reason.
- Blend: + Miéville (soft): habits as the carrier of a rule; offices known by their marks.
- How they combine: Grzędowicz picks which civic function is bent and why people keep an old custom; Miéville shows how that custom looks in a body and a form.
- Wins on conflict: Grzędowicz on the content of customs; Miéville on how they are shown.
- Not taken: Folk religion modelled on a real one, a society that is only a mirror of a real country.
- Atoms for this role: grzedowicz.world.2, grzedowicz.history.1, mieville.rules.1, mieville.institution.1

## Familiar institution, one element bent

- Id: grzedowicz.world.2        Binding: hard
- Effect: The reader judges the strange order by comparing it with an institution they already know, and so grasps its logic and its injustice without a glossary.
- Mechanism: (1) Pick a familiar civic function: licence, toll, queue, weights. (2) Keep its recognisable shape. (3) Change one load-bearing element: who pays, who may refuse, what counts as proof. (4) Show the effect on a person, not the principle. (5) Never name a real-world model.
- Test: Can a reader name the familiar institution and also say exactly which element is bent?
- In this world: Carrying licences, river tolls, weights and measures. The bent element is usually a leftover of the earlier rule that still binds one corner of the system.
- Plain: A porter needs a licence to carry over the bridge. The licence is renewed each spring at the weigh-house for a fee.
- Applied: The licence was renewed each spring at the weigh-house, for the fee and for the signature of whoever had held the same pitch the year before. Teodor had no one before him. The clerk took that to mean he had no pitch.
- Avoid: Allegory with a one-to-one real-world key. Explaining the bend in the narrator's voice. Naming a real institution or place.

## Practice outlives its reason

- Id: grzedowicz.history.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: An old custom feels real because people keep it without knowing why, and the reason, when it surfaces, is smaller or stranger than the custom.
- Mechanism: (1) Show the practice done correctly and without comment. (2) Put a wrong or partial folk reason in a character's mouth. (3) Later reveal the documentary reason: a cost, an accident, a quarrel. (4) The practice continues regardless.
- Test: Does a character keep a practice while the narrative later narrows or contradicts their reason for it?
- In this world: The premise itself. Each present rule is read through the earlier rule it replaced; a habit is the trace the earlier rule left in bodies.
- Plain: Porters touch the river-post before their first load. It was set up to mark the water level for the old toll, which was abolished long ago.
- Applied: Every porter touched the river-post before the first load. Kasia asked why. "For luck." The post had been set to read the water for the toll. The toll was gone, the mark worn flat, and still the hand went out.
- Avoid: A tidy reveal that explains everything. Mysticism without a documentary layer. Using real religious practices.

## The rule is lived before it is explained

- Id: mieville.rules.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: A prohibition feels binding because characters obey it automatically; the reader senses its weight through habit.
- Mechanism: (1) Show characters adjusting a movement or a glance without comment. (2) Give the adjustment a small cost. (3) A later scene shows what happens when someone fails to adjust. (4) Any explanation comes from a person who has reason to be brief.
- Test: Is the rule shown in someone's behaviour before any sentence states it?
- In this world: Porters' habits at certain doors, stairs and stalls, each tied to a rule that has since been replaced.
- Plain: Porters are not allowed to use the east door of the weigh-house.
- Applied: The porters came to the weigh-house in a line and at the east door each one angled a shoulder to the wall, as if the door were wet paint. Teodor, last in line, went straight at it and put his hand on the latch.
- Avoid: Making the rule the secret at the centre of the story; here it stays ordinary. Copying a source's central conceit or terms.

## The office known by its marks

- Id: mieville.institution.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: A powerful body feels real, and a little frightening, when it is met through its forms, marks and delays and seldom in person.
- Mechanism: (1) Show the body's trace: a stamp, a coloured thread, a form number. (2) People avoid asking where it sits. (3) When an official appears, they speak briefly and in procedure. (4) Its reach is shown by what is quietly not done.
- Test: Is the body shown by its traces and not by a description of its building or its leaders?
- In this world: The office that reconciles old rules and new, and handles whatever in the city is not natural. Its name and powers are for the pitch round.
- Plain: The Office of Old Measures was a powerful organisation in a large building by the river.
- Applied: The sack came back with a green thread through the neck. Nobody at the pitch asked who had put it there, and nobody unpicked it. At the weigh-house the clerk looked at the thread, and the fee was not asked.
- Avoid: A named secret police. Copying a source's enforcing body, its name or its function. Making the office omnipotent.
