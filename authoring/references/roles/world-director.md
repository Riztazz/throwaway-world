# world-director: technique cards

Status: proposed (the user approves the set by approving ANALYSIS.md and the sample pairs). Sample passages are illustrative and written for this world in English (D-0008); names and details in them are not canon and are not registered.

- Primary: Grzędowicz (hard): how the world is met and what is quietly bent in it.
- Blend: + Miéville (soft): how rules and offices appear, and the flat register for large facts.
- How they combine: Grzędowicz gives the way in (a newcomer decoding a familiar institution with one element bent). Miéville gives the register and the way rules and offices show up. The two stack: Grzędowicz for structure of discovery, Miéville for texture.
- Wins on conflict: Miéville on register and restraint, because the intake asks for a factual, restrained tone.
- Not taken: Spectacle, ornate description, a mission-of-destiny shape, any source's names, gear or scenes.
- Atoms for this role: grzedowicz.world.1, grzedowicz.world.2, grzedowicz.magic.1, grzedowicz.tone.1, mieville.rules.1, mieville.institution.1, mieville.tone.1

## Decoder at the threshold

- Id: grzedowicz.world.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: The reader learns the place at the pace, and for the price, that the newcomer pays for each fact, so the world feels lived in and not explained.
- Mechanism: (1) The viewpoint character is new to the place and has a job that forces contact with its rules. (2) A custom is met first as an obstacle: a refused load, a barred stair. (3) Someone gives a partial explanation, briefly. (4) The character acts on it and pays a small cost. (5) The full reason arrives later, or never. No local delivers a lecture.
- Test: Can you point to the line where the newcomer pays for a fact (time, coin, a wrong turn) before being given it?
- In this world: The novice porter meets each current regulation as a refusal at a gate; the older rule it replaced is found afterwards in the archive. The commission loop of the game has the same order: refusal, cost, partial answer, lookup.
- Plain: The foreman explained that porters may not cross the Salt Stair at noon. Long ago the ferry company closed it at high water. The company was gone, but the rule had stayed in the porters' book. Ewa nodded and went round the long way.
- Applied: The gate-clerk laid his stick across the stair. "Noon." Ewa had the sack on her back and the fee already counted. She went round by the tannery: forty minutes, a quarter of the fee. Later old Drozd said, not looking up, "Used to be the water. Ask the weigh-house what it is now."
- Avoid: A local who explains. A newcomer who turns out to be special; the mission-of-destiny shape (excluded). Copying any source's names, gear or scenes.

## Familiar institution, one element bent

- Id: grzedowicz.world.2        Binding: hard
- Effect: The reader judges the strange order by comparing it with an institution they already know, and so grasps its logic and its injustice without a glossary.
- Mechanism: (1) Pick a familiar civic function: licence, toll, queue, weights. (2) Keep its recognisable shape. (3) Change one load-bearing element: who pays, who may refuse, what counts as proof. (4) Show the effect on a person, not the principle. (5) Never name a real-world model.
- Test: Can a reader name the familiar institution and also say exactly which element is bent?
- In this world: Carrying licences, river tolls, weights and measures. The bent element is usually a leftover of the earlier rule that still binds one corner of the system.
- Plain: A porter needs a licence to carry over the bridge. The licence is renewed each spring at the weigh-house for a fee.
- Applied: The licence was renewed each spring at the weigh-house, for the fee and for the signature of whoever had held the same pitch the year before. Teodor had no one before him. The clerk took that to mean he had no pitch.
- Avoid: Allegory with a one-to-one real-world key. Explaining the bend in the narrator's voice. Naming a real institution or place.

## The marvel as a line item

- Id: grzedowicz.magic.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: A fantastic power reads as real because it arrives as a cost, a permit or a delay in the ordinary order, not as a display.
- Mechanism: (1) The power appears in the city's paperwork, prices or habits. (2) Its limit is shown through what someone could not do or had to pay. (3) The consequence falls on a bystander's work. (4) The effect itself is described only as far as a clerk would note it.
- Test: Is the power met first as an entry on a list, a price or a refusal, with no spectacle?
- In this world: "Fantasy in the background": a few things in the city are not natural and an office handles them. No magic system is fixed by this card; that is for the pitch round and a decision.
- Plain: A hedge-mage lifted the barrels onto the barge with a spell, and the porters watched in awe.
- Applied: Barrels above the third tier go by the lifter's mark, not by hand: the lifter's fee, and a day's wait until the lifter is free. Janek's crew carried four tiers that morning, and the lifter's mark stayed unused on the board.
- Avoid: Showy effects. Magic that solves the plot. A rule-set invented on the card.

## One material detail carries the mood

- Id: grzedowicz.tone.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: The atmosphere is felt through one concrete thing of the place and is not stated as a mood.
- Mechanism: (1) Choose one detail from the place's work or materials: tannery smell, a chain on a ferry stair. (2) Place it where the character's attention would land. (3) Let a practical consequence of the detail be what the scene turns on. (4) No mood adjectives.
- Test: Remove every mood word. Is the mood still there?
- In this world: The restraint rule caps this at one detail per scene, and the detail must cost something (a delay, a stain, a wet hand).
- Plain: It was a gloomy, cold dawn on the river and Marek felt uneasy.
- Applied: At dawn the ferry chain was still wet. It left a rust-coloured line across Marek's palm that would not wash off before the first weigh-in.
- Avoid: Piling up sensory detail; the source is described as rich and painterly, and that register is not taken (conflict A). Mood adjectives.

## The rule is lived before it is explained

- Id: mieville.rules.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: A prohibition feels binding because characters obey it automatically; the reader senses its weight through habit.
- Mechanism: (1) Show characters adjusting a movement or a glance without comment. (2) Give the adjustment a small cost. (3) A later scene shows what happens when someone fails to adjust. (4) Any explanation comes from a person who has reason to be brief.
- Test: Is the rule shown in someone's behaviour before any sentence states it?
- In this world: Porters' habits at certain doors, stairs and stalls, each tied to a rule that has since been replaced.
- Plain: Porters are not allowed to use the east door of the weigh-house.
- Applied: The porters came to the weigh-house in a line and at the east door each one angled a shoulder to the wall, as if the door were wet paint. Teodor, last in line, went straight at it and put his hand on the latch.
- Avoid: Making the rule the secret at the centre of the story; here it stays ordinary. Copying a source's central conceit or terms.

## The office known by its marks

- Id: mieville.institution.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: A powerful body feels real, and a little frightening, when it is met through its forms, marks and delays and seldom in person.
- Mechanism: (1) Show the body's trace: a stamp, a coloured thread, a form number. (2) People avoid asking where it sits. (3) When an official appears, they speak briefly and in procedure. (4) Its reach is shown by what is quietly not done.
- Test: Is the body shown by its traces and not by a description of its building or its leaders?
- In this world: The office that reconciles old rules and new, and handles whatever in the city is not natural. Its name and powers are for the pitch round.
- Plain: The Office of Old Measures was a powerful organisation in a large building by the river.
- Applied: The sack came back with a green thread through the neck. Nobody at the pitch asked who had put it there, and nobody unpicked it. At the weigh-house the clerk looked at the thread, and the fee was not asked.
- Avoid: A named secret police. Copying a source's enforcing body, its name or its function. Making the office omnipotent.

## Flat register for large facts

- Id: mieville.tone.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: Strange or grave conditions read as credible because the narration treats them as ordinary working facts.
- Mechanism: (1) State the large fact in the same sentence length and register as a delivery note. (2) Place it between two practical details. (3) Do not comment or evaluate. (4) The consequence is a form, a delay or a changed hour.
- Test: Is the gravest sentence of the scene as short as, or plainer than, the sentences around it?
- In this world: Flood, loss and the fantastic background are all written in the register of the weigh-house board.
- Plain: Horrifyingly, the river rose over the whole lower quarter in the night and drowned several families.
- Applied: The river took the lower quarter in the night. By morning the weigh-house had moved its hours to the upper stair, and the tally of those found went up on the board beside the ferry rates.
- Avoid: Sentimental flatness that is really a joke. Emotion deleted entirely. Copying a source's noir voice.
- Evidence: The City & The City (China Miéville; edition not given), no locator; not read. Type: memory only (the web fetch failed and no search was made). Not read: the text. Confidence: low. Spoiler ceiling: premise level only.
