# fiction-writer: technique cards

Status: proposed (the user approves the set by approving ANALYSIS.md and the sample pairs). Sample passages are illustrative and written for this world in English (D-0008); names and details in them are not canon and are not registered.

- Primary: Grzędowicz (hard): close viewpoint on a decoder, trade-based comparison, dodging dialogue, one mood detail.
- Blend: + Miéville (soft): rules shown in behaviour, case-against-rule scenes, flat register.
- How they combine: Three layers: Grzędowicz for scene shape and speech, Miéville for register, the intake's tone for the limit on both. Point of view: close third, past, open end (D-0006).
- Wins on conflict: Miéville on register (flat beats painterly). The intake's exclusions (no chosen one, no all-knowing narrator) beat both.
- Not taken: Painterly density, psychedelic mood shifts, a narrator who knows everything, any source's names or scenes.

## Decoder at the threshold

- Id: grzedowicz.world.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: The reader learns the place at the pace, and for the price, that the newcomer pays for each fact, so the world feels lived in and not explained.
- Mechanism: (1) The viewpoint character is new to the place and has a job that forces contact with its rules. (2) A custom is met first as an obstacle: a refused load, a barred stair. (3) Someone gives a partial explanation, briefly. (4) The character acts on it and pays a small cost. (5) The full reason arrives later, or never. No local delivers a lecture.
- Test: Can you point to the line where the newcomer pays for a fact (time, coin, a wrong turn) before being given it?
- In this world: The novice porter meets each current regulation as a refusal at a gate; the older rule it replaced is found afterwards in the archive. The commission loop of the game has the same order: refusal, cost, partial answer, lookup.
- Plain: The foreman explained that porters may not cross the Salt Stair at noon. Long ago the ferry company closed it at high water. The company was gone, but the rule had stayed in the porters' book. Ewa nodded and went round the long way.
- Applied: The gate-clerk laid his stick across the stair. "Noon." Ewa had the sack on her back and the fee already counted. She went round by the tannery: forty minutes, a quarter of the fee. Later old Drozd said, not looking up, "Used to be the water. Ask the weigh-house what it is now."
- Avoid: A local who explains. A newcomer who turns out to be special; the mission-of-destiny shape (excluded). Copying any source's names, gear or scenes.
- Evidence: Pan Lodowego Ogrodu (Fabryka Słów; edition not given), no locator; not read. ## Two scales in one scene

- Id: grzedowicz.world.3        Binding: hard
- Effect: A small present errand is read against an older, larger frame set beside it, so the reader sees both the errand and what it rests on.
- Mechanism: (1) Interleave the errand with a fragment from another scale: a ledger line, a rule's text, a record. (2) Let the fragment arrive uncommented. (3) Return to the errand with one shared word or object now carrying the fragment's weight. (4) Never state the connection.
- Test: Does one word or object appear in both layers, with the connection left unstated?
- In this world: In stories, an errand plus a citation from an old rule-book. In the game, a commission text plus an archive entry the player finds afterwards.
- Plain: Ola carried the crate to the weigh-house. The rule said crates must carry a seal. The rule had been made after a fire.
- Applied: Ola set the crate down at the weigh-house. The clerk pressed the seal into the wax. [Cooper's Row ledger, year of the third burning: Crates to bear the seal, that the owner be known when the owner is ash.] The wax was still warm when she lifted it.
- Avoid: A narrator who joins the two layers for the reader. Long quoted documents. Tying every scene to an archive.

## One material detail carries the mood

- Id: grzedowicz.tone.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: The atmosphere is felt through one concrete thing of the place and is not stated as a mood.
- Mechanism: (1) Choose one detail from the place's work or materials: tannery smell, a chain on a ferry stair. (2) Place it where the character's attention would land. (3) Let a practical consequence of the detail be what the scene turns on. (4) No mood adjectives.
- Test: Remove every mood word. Is the mood still there?
- In this world: The restraint rule caps this at one detail per scene, and the detail must cost something (a delay, a stain, a wet hand).
- Plain: It was a gloomy, cold dawn on the river and Marek felt uneasy.
- Applied: At dawn the ferry chain was still wet. It left a rust-coloured line across Marek's palm that would not wash off before the first weigh-in.
- Avoid: Piling up sensory detail; the source is described as rich and painterly, and that register is not taken (conflict A). Mood adjectives.

## Comparison from the trade

- Id: grzedowicz.prose.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: Description feels owned by the place because its comparisons come from the place's own work and materials.
- Mechanism: (1) For each comparison, pick from the character's trade or the city's materials: rope, water, ledger, brine. (2) Put it in one clause. (3) One per paragraph at most. (4) The comparison must be checkable: a length, a weight, a smell.
- Test: Could this comparison appear unchanged in a story set in any other city? If so, replace it.
- In this world: Comparisons come from carrying, weighing, the river, the tannery and the ledger.
- Plain: The old warehouse was huge and dark, like a cave.
- Applied: The warehouse ran back the length of a rope-walk, and in its dark the barrels stood shoulder to shoulder like a queue at the weigh-house.
- Avoid: Stock comparisons. Several comparisons in a row. Borrowing a source's images.

## The dodge carries the information

- Id: grzedowicz.dialogue.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: Speech shows rank, risk and knowledge through what a speaker avoids and where they steer.
- Mechanism: (1) The asker puts a plain question. (2) The answerer replies to a neighbouring question: price, place, person. (3) The omitted fact can be recovered from the shape of the dodge. (4) No tag explains the evasion.
- Test: Does the answer dodge the question and carry the information in the dodge?
- In this world: Clerks, foremen and old porters answer about tolls and hours when the real answer is about a rule.
- Plain: "Is the south gate open?" "No, it is closed because of the inspection."
- Applied: "Is the south gate open?" "The bridge toll is the same as yesterday." "That isn't what I asked." "Same as yesterday, I said."
- Avoid: Evasion that hides nothing. An explaining tag ("he said evasively"). Copying any source's exchanges.

## Understatement under pressure

- Id: grzedowicz.dialogue.2        Binding: hard
- Effect: Danger and cost are carried by dry understatement, so the scene stays factual and the reader supplies the weight.
- Mechanism: (1) A character states the hazard as a logistical fact. (2) Short sentence, no exclamation. (3) The listener answers with the next practical step. (4) Feeling shows only as behaviour: a pause, a tool put down.
- Test: Can the line be read aloud flat without losing its weight?
- In this world: Fits the tone "rzeczowy i powściągliwy". Used in emergencies on the river and at the gates.
- Plain: "The bridge is collapsing! We are all going to die!" he shouted.
- Applied: "Third span's going." He set the sack down. "Two at a time, and leave the carts."
- Avoid: Jokes that release the tension. Stoicism as a pose. Understatement in every line.

## The rule is lived before it is explained

- Id: mieville.rules.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: A prohibition feels binding because characters obey it automatically; the reader senses its weight through habit.
- Mechanism: (1) Show characters adjusting a movement or a glance without comment. (2) Give the adjustment a small cost. (3) A later scene shows what happens when someone fails to adjust. (4) Any explanation comes from a person who has reason to be brief.
- Test: Is the rule shown in someone's behaviour before any sentence states it?
- In this world: Porters' habits at certain doors, stairs and stalls, each tied to a rule that has since been replaced.
- Plain: Porters are not allowed to use the east door of the weigh-house.
- Applied: The porters came to the weigh-house in a line and at the east door each one angled a shoulder to the wall, as if the door were wet paint. Teodor, last in line, went straight at it and put his hand on the latch.
- Avoid: Making the rule the secret at the centre of the story; here it stays ordinary. Copying a source's central conceit or terms.

## Case against rule

- Id: mieville.rules.2        Binding: soft
- Effect: The plot moves by applying a rule to a specific case, and the reader learns the rule by watching where it fails to fit.
- Mechanism: (1) State the case: a load, a person, a document. (2) Apply the rule in a short exchange with an official. (3) The case fits the letter but not the intent, or the reverse. (4) The outcome is practical: a stamp, a refusal, a fine. (5) Next case.
- Test: Does each scene end with a ruling on a specific case?
- In this world: The shape of a commission in the game, and of a story built around one disputed load.
- Plain: "This crate is not allowed." "Why?" "Because it is not sealed."
- Applied: "Sealed?" "Sealed." The clerk turned the crate to the light. "Your seal is the new pattern, the crate is old stock. New seal on old stock is a transfer, and a transfer is the cooper's to sign."
- Avoid: Courtroom speeches. A clerk who is only an obstacle. Procedure that never produces a consequence.
- Evidence: The City & The City (China Miéville; edition not given), no locator; not read. Type: memory only (the web fetch failed and no search was made). Not read: the text. Confidence: low. Spoiler ceiling: premise level only.

## Flat register for large facts

- Id: mieville.tone.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: Strange or grave conditions read as credible because the narration treats them as ordinary working facts.
- Mechanism: (1) State the large fact in the same sentence length and register as a delivery note. (2) Place it between two practical details. (3) Do not comment or evaluate. (4) The consequence is a form, a delay or a changed hour.
- Test: Is the gravest sentence of the scene as short as, or plainer than, the sentences around it?
- In this world: Flood, loss and the fantastic background are all written in the register of the weigh-house board.
- Plain: Horrifyingly, the river rose over the whole lower quarter in the night and drowned several families.
- Applied: The river took the lower quarter in the night. By morning the weigh-house had moved its hours to the upper stair, and the tally of those found went up on the board beside the ferry rates.
- Avoid: Sentimental flatness that is really a joke. Emotion deleted entirely. Copying a source's noir voice.
- Evidence: The City & The City (China Miéville; edition not given), no locator; not read. Type: memory only (the web fetch failed and no search was made). Not read: the text. Confidence: low. Spoiler ceiling: premise level only.
