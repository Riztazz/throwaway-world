## One material detail carries the mood

- Id: grzedowicz.tone.1        Binding: hard
- Effect: The atmosphere is felt through one concrete thing of the place and is not stated as a mood.
- Mechanism: (1) Choose one detail from the place's work or materials: tannery smell, a chain on a ferry stair. (2) Place it where the character's attention would land. (3) Let a practical consequence of the detail be what the scene turns on. (4) No mood adjectives.
- Test: Remove every mood word. Is the mood still there?
- In this world: The restraint rule caps this at one detail per scene, and the detail must cost something (a delay, a stain, a wet hand).
- Plain: It was a gloomy, cold dawn on the river and Marek felt uneasy.
- Applied: At dawn the ferry chain was still wet. It left a rust-coloured line across Marek's palm that would not wash off before the first weigh-in.
- Avoid: Piling up sensory detail; the source is described as rich and painterly, and that register is not taken (conflict A). Mood adjectives.
- Evidence: Pan Lodowego Ogrodu (Fabryka Słów; edition not given), no locator; not read. Type: secondary commentary, namely publisher and reviewer summaries, Wikipedia and SF Encyclopedia snippets, read as search results (fetched summary, not the text), plus memory. Not read: the text, any volume. Confidence: low. Spoiler ceiling: premise level only (D-0007: everything the user has read, not verified).

## Flat register for large facts

- Id: mieville.tone.1        Binding: soft
- Effect: Strange or grave conditions read as credible because the narration treats them as ordinary working facts.
- Mechanism: (1) State the large fact in the same sentence length and register as a delivery note. (2) Place it between two practical details. (3) Do not comment or evaluate. (4) The consequence is a form, a delay or a changed hour.
- Test: Is the gravest sentence of the scene as short as, or plainer than, the sentences around it?
- In this world: Flood, loss and the fantastic background are all written in the register of the weigh-house board.
- Plain: Horrifyingly, the river rose over the whole lower quarter in the night and drowned several families.
- Applied: The river took the lower quarter in the night. By morning the weigh-house had moved its hours to the upper stair, and the tally of those found went up on the board beside the ferry rates.
- Avoid: Sentimental flatness that is really a joke. Emotion deleted entirely. Copying a source's noir voice.
- Evidence: The City & The City (China Miéville; edition not given), no locator; not read. Type: memory only (the web fetch failed and no search was made). Not read: the text. Confidence: low. Spoiler ceiling: premise level only.
