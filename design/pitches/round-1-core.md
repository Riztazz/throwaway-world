## A. The Two Books

**Name and hook.** Every gate in Łódź runs on two books: the posted rule, which the gate-clerk applies, and the kept rule, which a keeper (a guild, a shrine, an old family) still honours because they held the rule it replaced. Some loads only move under the kept rule, and a receiver can refuse a load that came through under the wrong book.

**In play or on the page.**
- Facts: a commission shows the load, the receiver and the districts to cross. At each gate the player sees the posted rule. At a fork the player chooses which book to carry under. A load is accepted or refused by the *receiver*, not fined by the city.
- Function: licences are issued by keepers. Each licence is the right to invoke one predecessor rule at named gates. That is the progression (D-0005): access, not levels.
- Cost: a licence from one keeper closes a door at a rival keeper whose account of the same history differs. Reading what a rule replaced at the archive costs time and a fee; the knowledge is yours afterwards and cannot be taken or lost.
- History: three keepers give three different accounts of what one rule replaced, attributed in the bible's manner ("the porters' book holds..."), with `Unknown:` where the world does not say.
- Central conflict (proposal): which account of the past the city's present rules rest on. Not a war; a settlement that holds as long as no keeper folds.
- Faction seeds (proposals): the weigh-house clerks (the posted book), the porters' book-keepers (custom), the remnant of a wound-up ferry company (liability and old routes), the river-shrine wardens (the oldest kept rules, least written down).
- Crowd effect: a keeper stays in the world as long as its book is carried. If players stop routing under a keeper's book, that keeper's standing falls and it eventually folds; its rule becomes archive-only for everyone. Players decide, by where they walk, which pasts survive.

**Why it differs.** The pressure is political and historical: a choice among existing accounts. B's pressure is physical volume, C's is procedure, D's is a timed upheaval.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows directly. The player is a decoder at the threshold of each gate (`world.1`), and the keepers are familiar institutions with one element bent (`world.2`); the dodge carries the information in every clerk's answer (`dialogue.1`). It does not take the mission or the outsider (conflict C of the reference map): a local novice learns the rule at the price of a fee or a favour.

Soft references: Miéville: used, for the rule lived before it is explained (`rules.1`) and the keeper known by its marks on a slip (`institution.1`). Left out: `rules.2` (case against rule), because there are no hearings in this pitch; disputes are settled by the receiver's refusal.

**Fit.** Fits D-0005, D-0009, D-0011 directly; the archive of old rules is the central system, not a side feature. It strains the "fantasy in the background" line least: the world is almost entirely institutional. It strains restraint on one point only: a keeper folding is the nearest thing to a spectacle, and is told as a notice board entry.

**What we give up.** Visible conflict between persons (no riots, no duels); the world is read, not fought. Biggest unknown: whether reading the archive feels like play or homework, and so how much the commissions themselves must teach.

**It unlocks.** (1) `tone-premise`: world-director. (2) A system spec `design/systems/two-books` (book choice at gates, licences, keeper standing): game-systems-designer. (3) The first `faction` page, the weigh-house: faction-designer, with the historian for its predecessor chain. Then `history` (protected; written only on approval).

**Shared world.**
- Personal: licences held, readings done, the job record.
- Party: a team carrying one load shares one book at each gate; if members hold different licences, the load moves under the book they all hold.
- Shared: which keepers stand or have folded, and which archive entries are attested.
- Late joiner: starts with the weigh-house's posted book only. The archive shows every rule with its dated chain of predecessors and any keeper that has already folded, so a late joiner can read the whole past but still pays for it.

## B. The Crowd Is the Weather

**Name and hook.** The bridges, stairs and weigh-houses of Łódź have a capacity, and the porters are the load on them. The city does not legislate; it patches the last jam, and each patch pushes the older rule into the archive together with the jam that caused it.

**In play or on the page.**
- Facts: each chokepoint (a bridge, a stair, a gate) has a queue that every player can read at its board. A commission is routed with that queue in view.
- Function: when a chokepoint jams repeatedly, the office that owns it posts a patch drawn from a short list the world fixes per chokepoint (an hour limit, a toll, a mark on the slip, a one-way order). Players learn the family of patches. Progression (D-0005): licences are exemptions from patches, and routes are the unpatched ways round.
- Cost: success is punished. A route that works for many players gets patched shut. The patch always arrives after the crowd, so a veteran's best route is the one nobody else has found yet.
- History: every current rule has a recorded jam behind it; the archive gives rule, predecessor, the jam that replaced it, and who still keeps the old one (the voice.md rule entry).
- Central conflict (proposal): the offices that own the chokepoints compete for the traffic and its toll revenue, while the river wardens want the water clear.
- Faction seeds (proposals): the bridge-office (tolls), the weigh-house (order and seals), the stair-guild (a porters' body that owns one stair), the river wardens.
- Crowd effect: the crowd is the pressure itself. Where players go, rules appear.

**Why it differs.** The pressure is volume, not a choice among pasts. Rules come from the crowd's own traffic and nobody has to hold a grudge about them.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows through practice outliving its reason (`history.1`: a patch stays long after its jam is gone) and two scales in one scene (`world.3`: one porter's carry against the city's flow, both visible on the board). The decoder effect (`world.1`) shows only in reading the board. It cannot carry the world-building depth of the source: this pitch has the thinnest history of the four, because the history is generated by play.

Soft references: Miéville: used for the flat register of the notice board (`tone.1`: large facts told as a patch order). Left out: `rules.2` (no cases), `institution.1` (offices are known by what they own, not by marks), the reason being that this pitch is mechanical rather than procedural.

**Fit.** Fits D-0009 (shared = city-level rule changes) better than any other pitch. It strains D-0005 slightly: progression by knowledge of old rules is weaker, because the old rule is a record of a past jam, not a living claim. Fantasy in the background appears only as a cargo class that behaves oddly, handled as a line in a rate table (`magic.1`).

**What we give up.** Authored history and strong factions. Biggest unknown: an emergent system needs a crowd. Low-population shards would have no jams and so no rules. Fix on offer: NPC porters supply a baseline load (a proposal that costs content and needs the engine team's agreement).

**It unlocks.** (1) `tone-premise`: world-director. (2) A system spec `design/systems/chokepoints-and-patches` (queues, patch families, exemptions): game-systems-designer. (3) The first `location` page, the busiest bridge: location-designer, with the geographer-ecologist for the river it crosses.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: licences and exemptions held, the job record.
- Party: a shared route and a shared load; the slowest chokepoint sets the party's pace.
- Shared: queue state at every chokepoint, and the patches in force.
- Late joiner: arrives in the city as it stands, queues and all. The archive shows each patch with the jam that made it, so the late joiner can see why a rule exists even if the jam never happened in their time.

## C. The Docket

**Name and hook.** A delivery can be contested by the receiver, by a keeper, or by the city. A contested load goes to the Docket, where a clerk rules by the rule as it stands and cites the one it replaced. A rule in Łódź means what the last ruling said.

**In play or on the page.**
- Facts: the player carries a slip. When a delivery is contested, the player brings what the player has: the slip, the seal, a witness (another player or an NPC porter), a reading from the archive.
- Function: a hearing is a short, procedural scene. The player answers the clerk's questions; the clerk rules and the ruling is entered in the register. After enough consistent rulings (the threshold is an engine value, not set here), the register posts the ruling as a rule and the earlier rule goes to the archive. Progression (D-0005): standing with the Docket (the right to bring a case, to cite a predecessor) and the archive knowledge to cite.
- Cost: hearings take time at the Docket, a fee, and a mark on the job record if lost. Citing a predecessor rule that no clerk honours can count against the player.
- History: the register is itself the history; each rule has its list of rulings.
- Central conflict (proposal): the receiving houses want strict rule and fast delivery; the porters' custom wants the benefit of the doubt; the Docket wants rulings that can be repeated.
- Faction seeds (proposals): the Docket clerks, the receiving houses, the porters' book, a keeper house that appears only as a party to cases.
- Crowd effect: the crowd makes law by bringing cases. Rules harden where many porters bring the same case.

**Why it differs.** The unit of play is the case, not the route. Rules come from rulings, which makes the pitch procedural where A is political and B is mechanical.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows only in the lines and in decoding (`dialogue.1`: the clerk's dodge in a hearing; `world.1`: the player learns the rule through a ruling). It cannot supply the structure: the hard reference has no case-and-ruling shape. Per the reference map's blend (quest-narrative-designer), Miéville wins on structure here and Grzędowicz supplies the lines.

Soft references: Miéville: used heavily, for case against rule (`rules.2`) and the office known by its marks (`institution.1`: a ruling's stamp on a slip). Left out: nothing, but this pitch leans on the soft reference more than the hard one, which is its main weakness against D-0003.

**Fit.** Fits D-0005 (knowledge of old rules is the evidence you cite) and D-0009. It strains D-0003 as above, and it strains the exclusion of a chosen-one plot: a player whose case becomes law can feel special. Mitigation: rulings are entered by the office, never in a player's name.

**What we give up.** Speed and spatial play: the Docket is a room, and most of the loop would be talking. Biggest unknown: whether hearings can be shared among simultaneous players without queueing, and the amount of procedural text each rule needs.

**It unlocks.** (1) `tone-premise`: world-director. (2) A system spec `design/systems/the-docket` (cases, evidence, rulings, the register): game-systems-designer, with the quest-narrative-designer for the first hearing. (3) The first `faction` page, the Docket clerks: faction-designer.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: the case record and the archive readings.
- Party: a joint case; the party testifies together and shares the ruling.
- Shared: the register of rulings and the rules it has posted.
- Late joiner: the register is public. The first case a late joiner sits is a scripted plain case, the same for everyone, that teaches how a ruling cites a predecessor.

## D. The Turn (wild card)

**Name and hook.** Łódź runs under one of several complete rule-sets. When the river-house's levy is met, the city turns to the next one: the rule-set every current rule once replaced, and the one the current rules will later replace. The cause is `Unknown:`, and the price is a levy on each porter.

**In play or on the page.**
- Facts: a regime is a full list of rules for gates, hours and weights. The levy is a posted fee and a counter that everyone can read. Between turns the city is stable and the rules are learnable.
- Function: when the Turn comes, every licence tied to the outgoing regime lapses. Knowledge does not lapse: what a player has read stays read, and in the incoming regime it is the player's advantage. Progression (D-0005): knowledge of the old rules is the only thing that carries over.
- Cost: loss of access at each Turn. The levy is paid by porters, so the crowd pays to end its own regime.
- History: the regimes are the layers of the city's past; each Turn is a dated entry. Fantasy in the background is the Turn itself, told as a price list, never as an event (`magic.1`).
- Central conflict (proposal): the offices of the standing regime want the levy unmet; the keepers of the dormant regime want it met.
- Faction seeds (proposals): the river-house (collects the levy), the standing regime's offices, the keepers of the dormant regime, a porters' body that profits from the lapse (sells its members the re-licensing route).
- Crowd effect: the crowd decides when the Turn comes, by paying the levy.

**Why it differs.** The only pitch with upheaval. Rules are not replaced one at a time; whole sets are replaced, and replaced rules come back.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows strongly: the marvel as a line item (`magic.1`), a familiar institution with one element bent (`world.2`, the levy office), two scales in one scene (`world.3`, a porter at a gate while the city turns). It does not take the scale of gods' wars or any lush description: the Turn is a fee schedule.

Soft references: Miéville: used for the flat register of the announcement (`tone.1`) and the office known by its marks (`institution.1`). Left out: `rules.2`, because there are no hearings.

**Fit.** Strains the most. It strains the tone ("practical consequence over spectacle": a city-wide turn is the nearest thing to a spectacle in any pitch), D-0005 (progression by access, which the Turn deletes), and the exclusion of a chosen-one plot (a crowd that triggers the Turn can feel like the chosen). The reference map's conflict B says fantasy is background, and a Turn is foreground unless it is told only as a levy.

**What we give up.** Stable investment in licences, and the quiet tone. Biggest unknown: whether players accept periodic loss of access, and what a late joiner who never sees a Turn live makes of the regime table.

**It unlocks.** (1) `tone-premise`: world-director. (2) A `magic-system` page for the Turn (protected, core-rule type; drafted and shown only): magic-fusion. (3) The `history` ledger of regimes: historian.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: knowledge of old rules; licences (which lapse).
- Party: a shared route; each member's licences apply separately at a gate.
- Shared: the levy counter, the current regime, the dates of the Turns.
- Late joiner: enters the regime in force. The archive holds the full regime table, with the dated Turns, so a late joiner can read every regime but must pay to act under any but the current one.

## Comparison

| | A. Two Books | B. Crowd Is the Weather | C. The Docket | D. The Turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source of rules | choice among existing pasts | the crowd's traffic | rulings on cases | the cycle of regimes |
| Unit of play | route and book choice | route and queue | hearing | route under a regime |
| Hard reference | strong | medium | weak on structure | strong, but strains tone |
| Soft reference | used | used lightly | leaned on | used lightly |
| Strains | little | D-0005 (a little) | D-0003, chosen-one risk | tone, D-0005, chosen-one |
| Needs from engine | a standing counter per keeper | live queue state, NPC baseline load | hearing flow for a crowd | a shared counter and a lapse of licences |
| Authored history | rich | thin | medium | rich |

No pitch strains an exclusion so hard that a constraint needs changing. D strains tone enough that choosing it deserves a deliberate yes.

## Recommendation

**A, The Two Books.** It is the pitch where the premise ("which old rule each one replaced") is the thing the player does every session, not a background fact. It fits all accepted decisions with the least strain, it uses the hard reference most directly, and it has the most authored history for the later rounds to build on.

One merge worth considering: **A with B's trigger.** Keeper standing in A would then fall not only with players' choices of book but with the jams at the gates that keeper's book governs. Seam: A and B would share one signal (traffic per route). The cost is that A inherits B's need for live queue state and its low-population problem.

Pitches I would not choose, and why:
- **D**: it strains the tone and deletes the progression the accepted decisions describe. Its best idea, the marvel as a fee, can go into A as the way fantasy shows up.
- **C** as the core: it leans on the soft reference more than the hard one and makes the loop talk. Its hearing is a good *quest form* inside A, for the contested-delivery case, if the user wants it later.
- **B** as the core: sound, but the history is thinnest and a small population breaks it.
