## A. The Reach

**Name and hook.** One stretch of the Żółwia, from the shrine weir upstream to the Yard Bridge downstream, walked in about an hour, with its two banks, its stairs and its landings. The first pages describe this and nothing else; the rest of the city is a set of named gates on its edge that do not open yet.

**In play or on the page.**
- Scale: end to end about an hour on foot along the water; crossing the river takes minutes at a bridge and longer by the ferry. The commission loop uses all of it: a load can start at one end and need a day of reading rules to reach the other.
- Districts (proposals, four): the **near bank** (weigh-house stand, job board, grain-house), the **far bank** (guild halls, the ferry landing, the wound-up guild's hall), the **lower stair** (porters' stalls, the porters' book) and the **weir** (the river-shrine and its approach).
- Edges: upstream, the shrine weir, past which the river is the shrine wardens' ground and loads do not go; downstream, the Yard Bridge and the weigh-house stand, past which the city goes on and the gate is posted "not yet". Landward, the city wall's inner street: a named gate to each district that is not in this part.
- Physical constraints (proposals): the river is too wide to carry across and too deep to ford, so crossings are bridges (tolled), a ferry (the ferry company's old right) and the weir (closed by the shrine). It floods in a season, which closes the lower stair first.
- Cast placement: Halina Brzoza on the Yard Bridge; Teodor Mróz at the lower stair; Irena Szalas at the ferry landing; Kuba Wiślak at the weir; Lucjan Fedak at the grain-house, near bank; Marek Gil in the archive, far bank; Wincenty Zając in the wound-up guild's hall, far bank; Dorota Wilk anywhere on the lower stair or the bridge.
- Central conflict in place: each crossing belongs to a different keeper, so the route a load takes is a choice of whose past it passes through.

**Why it differs.** The only pitch where the whole first part is one continuous stretch of water, so every district is in sight of at least one other.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows directly through two scales in one scene (`world.3`: a porter at a gate with the whole reach visible from it) and one material detail carrying the mood (`tone.1`: the weir's water as the shrine's mark). It does not take a planet-scale mirror of Earth; the reach is a place, not a world.

Soft references: Miéville: used for the office known by its marks (`institution.1`: each crossing marked by its keeper) and the rule lived before explained (`rules.1`). Left out: the soft reference's central conceit of two overlapping cities, which the reference map already leaves out.

**Fit.** Fits D-0002 exactly, D-0005 and the cast. It strains one thing: the river is a clean organising device, and the political layout (each crossing a keeper's) is tidy. The geographer must give it ordinary untidy features (a tannery, a ford nobody uses) so it does not read as engineered.

**What we give up.** The sense of a big city. Biggest unknown: whether a single reach holds enough commissions for the loop to stay varied; the gates "not yet" are the pressure valve.

**It unlocks.** (1) `geography`: the Reach, with the weir, banks and flood season: geographer-ecologist. (2) `location`: the Yard Bridge and its weigh-house stand: location-designer. (3) An SVG atlas of the reach: cartographer, from the first geography page.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: routes and gates the player knows, the readings bought.
- Party: a shared route along the reach and a shared load.
- Shared: which crossings are open, the flood state, a keeper's fold.
- Late joiner: starts at the job board on the near bank, with the weigh-house's posted book only. The archive and the gates "not yet" are signposted at the first board.

## B. The Bridgehead

**Name and hook.** One crossing, the Yard Bridge, and the approaches either side of it, described to the grain of the stone. Everything that happens in the first part happens within sight of the weigh-house stand.

**In play or on the page.**
- Scale: ten minutes' walk across, from the near bank's grain-house to the far bank's stair. The loop's "route through districts" becomes a route through stands, stairs and landings of the one crossing.
- Districts (proposals, three): the **upper cart-way**, the **lower walk** and the **stair**. These are the three levels of one bridge, not separate quarters.
- Edges: the approach streets on each bank, each ending at a gate marked with a keeper's sign that does not open yet. The rest of the city is told by what porters say.
- Physical constraints (proposals): one bridge on three levels, sited at a narrowing of the river; flood closes the stair.
- Cast placement: Halina on the lower walk; Teodor at the foot of the stair; Irena at a landing under the arch; Kuba is reached only by a long trip off the edge; Lucjan at the grain-house; Marek's archive is a day-trip off the edge; Wincenty's hall at the far end of the stair; Dorota anywhere on the bridge.
- Central conflict in place: the example rule from round 2 (the upper cart-way closed to loaded porters) is the place's standing argument.

**Why it differs.** Smallest and deepest: one place, three levels, thin on distance.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows through one material detail carrying the mood (`tone.1`: the worn lower walk) and two scales in one scene (`world.3`: a porter on the walk, carts above). The decoder at the threshold (`world.1`) shows at the bridge's gates only. It cannot carry a world to decode: the place is too small to hold history beyond the bridge.

Soft references: Miéville: used for the office known by its marks (`institution.1`: the stamp on the stand). Left out: `rules.1` as a district-wide habit, because one bridge is too small for it to vary from place to place.

**Fit.** Fits D-0005 in spirit but strains it in scale: "route through districts" becomes a walk along a bridge. It strains D-0002 on the lower side (an hour's walk is the limit, and this uses a fraction). It also puts Kuba and Marek off the edge, which breaks the cast from round 2.

**What we give up.** Most of the cast's ground, and routes long enough to need a decision. Biggest unknown: whether ten minutes of ground holds hours of play.

**It unlocks.** (1) `location`: the Yard Bridge: location-designer. (2) `faction`: the weigh-house: faction-designer. (3) `geography`: the narrowing and the flood: geographer-ecologist.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: routes along the bridge's levels, the readings bought.
- Party: a shared load across the levels.
- Shared: which levels are closed, the stand's hours.
- Late joiner: starts on the near-bank approach with the lower walk's posted book.

## C. The Whole Ring

**Name and hook.** The city in outline: seven districts, each a few paragraphs, and the hour's walk of fields and roads around it. The first pages name every district; none is deep.

**In play or on the page.**
- Scale: the city across in about an hour on foot; the surroundings out to the hour's walk beyond the wall, listed as villages, a quarry and the toll road.
- Districts (proposals, seven): the river reach (as in A), the weigh-house quarter, the grain-house quarter, the guild hall quarter, the ferry quay, the old town round the shrine, and the outer ring beyond the wall.
- Edges: the hour's walk from the wall, marked on the toll road by the last stone.
- Physical constraints (proposals): the river bisects the city; the old town stands on the higher ground; the outer ring is farmland and one quarry.
- Cast placement: all eight, one or two to a district.
- Central conflict in place: each district keeps its own mix of posted and kept books, so a long route is a long argument.

**Why it differs.** The only pitch that spends the whole D-0002 scale on breadth. It reads like a city; it is thin everywhere.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows through the decoder at the threshold (`world.1`: each district is a new set of rules to pay for). It cannot show the layered, lived-in depth of the source: with seven districts, none has room for a layer.

Soft references: Miéville: used for the flat register of the district list (`tone.1`). Left out: `institution.1` per district, because seven sets of marks are too many to design at this stage.

**Fit.** Fits D-0002 most literally and the commission loop's "districts" best. It strains the pitch prompt's advice to keep the first part small, and strains the cast: the eight are spread too thinly to meet each other. Seven district pages plus seven sets of rules is the largest first workload of the four.

**What we give up.** Depth; a quiet first place. Biggest unknown: whether seven thin districts feel like a city or like a list.

**It unlocks.** (1) `geography`: the whole ring: geographer-ecologist. (2) Seven `location` stubs: location-designer. (3) A first `faction` page: faction-designer.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: districts visited, rules learned per district.
- Party: a shared long route.
- Shared: which districts' gates are open, closures, a keeper's fold.
- Late joiner: starts at the wall gate nearest the job board; the district list is on the gate-board.

## D. The Plates (wild card)

**Name and hook.** Above the Yard Bridge the Żółwia splits and runs round a chain of islands, the Plates. Each island is the ground of one keeper, and each bridge between them is a toll in that keeper's name.

**In play or on the page.**
- Scale: five islands, crossed in an hour on foot including the bridges; the near and far banks are the mainland edges.
- Districts (proposals, five): one island per keeper. The weigh-house island, the porters' island (the lower stair and the book), the ferry island (the landing and the ferry company's old yards), the shrine island (the weir), and the wound-up guild's island, half deserted.
- Edges: the mainland at both ends, where the posted book is applied; the water on every other side.
- Physical constraints (proposals): the islands are silt bars cut by the channels; the channels carry the flood in turn; the shortest crossings close first.
- Cast placement: each keeper on their own island; Lucjan Fedak and Marek Gil on the mainland banks; Dorota Wilk on whichever bridge she is working.
- Central conflict in place: a route is a sequence of islands, so the choice of book at each bridge is also the choice of which island's rule to cross.

**Why it differs.** The only pitch where the politics is drawn on the map. A route is a path through rival jurisdictions with water between them.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows through a familiar place with one element bent (`world.2`: a river city whose parts are kept apart by water and rule) and the decoder at the threshold (`world.1`: every bridge a toll). It cannot take the source's scale; the Plates are a small set piece.

Soft references: Miéville: used for the office known by its marks (`institution.1`: each island's marker). Left out: any overlap or concealment between the islands, which would copy the source's central conceit.

**Fit.** Strains the most. It makes the core's political layout literal (one keeper, one island), which can read as a theme park; it strains D-0004 only if the geography looks designed to the plot rather than to the river. Folded keepers leave an island without a master, which is a strong visible effect and the nearest this place comes to spectacle. A keeper's fold costs a whole island's pages.

**What we give up.** Plausibility; the river's untidiness. Biggest unknown: whether players read the islands as a place or as a menu of factions.

**It unlocks.** (1) `geography`: the Plates and the channels: geographer-ecologist. (2) `faction`: the weigh-house with its island: faction-designer. (3) An atlas of the five islands: cartographer.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: islands visited, readings bought on each.
- Party: a shared route across bridges; each member pays each toll.
- Shared: which bridges are open, which island's keeper stands.
- Late joiner: starts on the near bank; the archive shows an island's keeper and its folded predecessors.

## Recommendation

**A, The Reach.** It uses D-0002's hour fully, holds all eight cast members within sight of each other, gives the core's keepers each a crossing without drawing the politics as a map, and gives the commission loop routes long enough to need a decision. The named gates "not yet" on its edge hold the rest of the city in reserve at no page cost.

One merge worth considering: **A with C's district list as the signage on the "not yet" gates.** Seam: the seven names from C become only the names on the gates, with no pages, so the city beyond is promised and not designed. Cost: seven names are on record as the city's shape, and the city's later regions are partly fixed by them.

What I would not choose, and why:
- **B**: it breaks the round 2 cast (Kuba and Marek fall off the edge) and uses a fraction of D-0002's scale.
- **C**: spends the largest first workload for the thinnest result.
- **D**: a good map, but it draws the core's politics on the ground and risks reading as a theme park. If A holds, the Plates could be a later district upstream of the weir.
