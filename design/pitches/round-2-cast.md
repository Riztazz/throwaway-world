## A. Three Accounts

**Name and hook.** Seven people, each of whom holds one account of why the upper cart-way is closed, and none of whom will say it plainly. A player who talks to all of them has the whole disagreement and no one's confession.

**In play or on the page.**

| Working name, place | Wants | Would never | Function | Cost / limit | Story |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Halina Brzoza**, gate-clerk at the weigh-house stand on the Yard Bridge | Her slips to balance at the bell | Stamp a seal she has not read | The first posted-book contact. Gives the ledger's account: a licence fee | Answers only the nearest practical question | yes |
| **Teodor Mróz**, keeper of the porters' book, stall at the lower stair | His book copied by someone who can read the margins | Lend it out of the stall | The porters' account: the cart-way closed after a collapse. Sells readings by the page | Takes work, not coin, for the margin notes | no |
| **Irena Szalas**, last signing agent of a wound-up ferry company | The old ferry tariffs honoured at one crossing | Sign for a load she cannot trace | The ferry account: closure protected the ferry's trade. Issues the ferry licence | Her licence closes a door at the weigh-house | yes |
| **Kuba Wiślak**, warden of the river-shrine | That his rule is never written down | Take coin for a reading | The oldest kept rule; gives no reason, only a time and a place | A reading costs a day's carrying on his errands | no |
| **Lucjan Fedak**, factor at a grain-house (a receiver) | The load accepted, on time, under a book he knows | Accept a short load; explain a refusal | The authority who refuses. His refusal is the penalty for the wrong book | Cannot be argued with; only re-routed around | no |
| **Dorota Wilk**, veteran porter holding two keepers' licences | To stop being between them | Carry for either keeper in front of the other | A party-mate for hire who knows which gate takes which book; sells that knowledge | Charges for every tip; is not a mentor and does not rate the player | yes |
| **Marek Gil**, archive reader at the city archive | The chain of predecessors for every rule complete | Remove an entry, even one he thinks is false | The reading fee-taker; his archive attests entries as more players read them | Slow; will not summarise | yes |

- Central conflict through people: the three accounts cannot all be true, and the settlement holds as long as nobody makes them say so.
- Crowd effect: the seven are the fixed faces of A's keeper counters. If players stop carrying under Irena's or Teodor's book, that person's standing falls and the person eventually folds (a notice, not a death).

**Why it differs.** The cast is arranged by what each person *believes about the past*. B arranges it by place on a route, C by cost, D by trade in falsehood.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows directly. Practice outliving its reason is each keeper in turn (`history.1`); the dodge carries the information in every answer (`dialogue.1`); the understatement-under-pressure atom (`dialogue.2`) shows when a keeper's book is threatened. It does not take an outsider's special status: the player is one porter among many.

Soft references: Miéville is not assigned to character-designer in the reference map, so it is left out of the characters. It appears only in the offices around them (Halina's stamp, Marek's entry), per `institution.1`.

**Fit.** Fits D-0005, D-0009, D-0011 and the exclusions. It strains one thing: seven people each with a hidden "account" can read as a puzzle with a single answer. The bible must keep `Unknown:` honest; no cast member may turn out to be right.

**What we give up.** Variety of motive: the cast is about knowledge and access, not love, debt or ambition. Biggest unknown: whether players tell the seven apart by what they know rather than by what they say.

**It unlocks.** (1) `faction`: the weigh-house: faction-designer, with historian. (2) `character`: Halina Brzoza and Teodor Mróz as the two ends of the disagreement: character-designer. (3) `faction`: the porters' book: culture-builder. Naming of all seven comes before any page: naming-specialist.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: what each NPC has told this player, the readings and licences bought from them.
- Party: Dorota can be hired by a party for a route; she holds her own licences.
- Shared: each keeper's standing and whether they have folded; what Marek's archive has attested.
- Late joiner: meets all seven, standing as they are today. A keeper who folded before the player arrived stands in the archive as a closed entry with a dated notice; the archive tells a late joiner who they were and what rule left with them.

## B. The Route

**Name and hook.** Seven people, met in the order of one long commission from the job board to the receiving door. Each is a threshold: nothing is learned from them without a price paid at the last one.

**In play or on the page.**

| Working name, place | Wants | Would never | Function | Cost / limit | Story |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bronek Zawada**, broker at the job board | His commissions taken before the bell | Name the receiver before the porter signs | Gives the commission and the first rule on it | Keeps back which book the load needs | no |
| **Halina Brzoza**, gate-clerk (as in A) | Her slips to balance | Stamp an unread seal | The posted rule at the first gate | Nearest-question answers only | yes |
| **Stach Kmita**, foreman of the stair-guild | His stair run by his own hours | Let a rival guild's porter on the stair | Controls the one shortcut | A fee in work, paid before the climb | no |
| **Irena Szalas**, ferry-remnant agent (as in A) | The old tariff honoured | Sign for an untraced load | The ferry licence and the river leg | Her licence closes a door | yes |
| **Wanda Pstrąg**, runner who sells warnings | Small coin and fewer questions | Sell the same warning twice | Early notice of a gate's mood | A warning can be wrong and is never refunded | yes |
| **Lucjan Fedak**, grain-house factor (as in A) | The load accepted | Accept a short load | Accepts or refuses at the door | No appeal | no |
| **Kuba Wiślak**, shrine warden (as in A) | His rule unwritten | Take coin | Appears only on the longest route | A reading costs a day of errands | no |

- Central conflict through people: each person stands between the porter and the door and owes the next one.
- Crowd effect: players meet the same seven in the same order; popular routes wear their thresholds down (a clerk's short temper, a stair run late).

**Why it differs.** The cast is arranged by *where in a trip you meet them*. Their interior lives are thin; their value is the order.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows directly through the decoder at the threshold (`world.1`): every person is a gate at which a fact is paid for. The dodge (`dialogue.1`) shows in each short exchange. It cannot show depth of character: the atoms assigned to this role (`history.1`, `dialogue.2`) have almost no room in a person who is met once.

Soft references: Miéville is not assigned to character-designer and is left out of the characters; the offices they hold (the stamp, the board) are Miéville's `institution.1`, as in round 1.

**Fit.** Fits the commission loop (D-0005) best of the four. It strains the cast brief: a route of seven is a level layout, and most seeds cannot carry a short story. It also puts two A characters in a second pitch, so merging is cheap.

**What we give up.** Individuals; the cast is mostly furniture. Biggest unknown: whether players remember people met once in transit.

**It unlocks.** (1) `location`: the Yard Bridge: location-designer. (2) `character`: Wanda Pstrąg, the one new seed: character-designer. (3) `faction`: the stair-guild: faction-designer.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: who the player has paid, the warnings bought.
- Party: a party shares the route; each member pays each threshold separately.
- Shared: the thresholds' standing (the stair-guild's hours, a clerk's temper).
- Late joiner: starts at the job board with Bronek; the rest are met in order and written up in the archive as they come.

## C. Who Pays

**Name and hook.** Seven people the rules have cost something, and the person each cost went to. The player meets them as work (a refused load, a debt, a lost licence), not as officials.

**In play or on the page.**

| Working name, place | Wants | Would never | Function | Cost / limit | Story |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Józef Lis**, porter refused at a gate for the wrong book | To carry again under the book he trained on | Beg the clerk | Shows what a refusal costs; gives a shared load if the party takes his book | Pride; will not accept charity | yes |
| **Agata Rogal**, factor whose load was refused and spoiled | The loss made good | Blame the porter aloud | A receiver with a grievance; a commission to reroute her stock | Pays late, and says so | yes |
| **Ludwik Sęk**, retired clerk who wrote one of the present rules | The rule to outlast him | Admit he does not recall the reason | Gives the weigh-house's side of a change | His account is the most polished and the least checkable | yes |
| **Hanna Dzik**, widow of a porter lost at the collapse | A name on the stair-guild's board | Ask the guild for money | The collapse as a person, not a date | A reading from her is a favour owed | no |
| **Olek Bąk**, runner, the youngest on the lower walk | A licence of his own | Carry a load he cannot read the seal of | Sells shortcuts; costs one favour | Tests the player's patience, not their purse | no |
| **Wincenty Zając**, last clerk of a wound-up guild | His guild's book carried by anyone | Sell the book | A keeper whose book is falling out of use: the crowd decides whether it folds | Only a porter's route keeps him standing | yes |
| **Maria Kruk**, weigh-house inspector | Her rounds to end without a refusal | Look away from a wrong seal | The enforcer; stops a load on sight | Cannot be argued, only avoided | yes |

- Central conflict through people: no one in the seven is wrong; each is paying for someone else's settlement.
- Crowd effect: Wincenty's standing is the readable form of the crowd's choices; the player can see a keeper fold.

**Why it differs.** The cast is arranged by *cost*. Every seed is a consequence of a rule, so the pitch is the most direct source of stories (five of seven can carry one).

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows through understatement under pressure (`dialogue.2`: danger as a logistical fact, then the next step) and practice outliving its reason (`history.1`: Ludwik's rule). The dodge (`dialogue.1`) shows when Ludwik is asked for the reason. Confidence on `dialogue.2` is very low per the reference map; an excerpt would raise it.

Soft references: Miéville is not assigned to character-designer and is left out of the characters.

**Fit.** Fits D-0006 (stories) best. It strains the tone: a widow and a spoiled load invite sentiment, against "practical consequences over spectacle". Mitigation: every sentence states a cost, not a feeling (`voice.md`, "no mood adjectives"). It also strains D-0011 slightly: a refused porter is a mirror of the player and can feel like a warning aimed at them.

**What we give up.** The seven are less connected to the four factions than in A (three of them are customers or bystanders). Biggest unknown: whether players want to meet people who lost, when the loop is about getting paid.

**It unlocks.** (1) `character`: Józef Lis and Agata Rogal as the two sides of a refusal: character-designer. (2) `faction`: the weigh-house: faction-designer. (3) A first short story outline from Wincenty Zając: fiction-writer, in `drafts/stories/`.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: favours owed and received, the loads taken from them.
- Party: Józef can join a party only if every member carries under his book.
- Shared: Wincenty's standing and whether he has folded.
- Late joiner: meets them all as they stand. A fold that happened earlier is a dated entry in the archive, with who the person was.

## D. The Reason-Writers (wild card)

**Name and hook.** In Łódź a rule that has lost its reason can have one written for it, for a fee, in a hand the archive will take. Seven people pay for, write, sell or catch invented predecessors, and no one in the game's text says which entries are real.

**In play or on the page.**

| Working name, place | Wants | Would never | Function | Cost / limit | Story |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Zofia Czapla**, scrivener at the archive's side door | Her fee paid before she writes | Write for a rival the same night | Writes predecessors for pay; the player can commission one | A false entry can be found out at a cost to the buyer | yes |
| **Marek Gil**, archive reader (as in A) | The chain of predecessors complete | Remove an entry | Tries to catch her entries by comparing hands | Cannot prove, only flag | yes |
| **Halina Brzoza**, gate-clerk (as in A) | Her slips to balance | Stamp an unread seal | Applies the rule as the archive gives it | Has to take the archive's word | yes |
| **Teodor Mróz**, keeper of the porters' book (as in A) | His book kept honest | Have a margin changed | Holds the one account no scrivener can touch | Slow to verify anything | no |
| **Lucjan Fedak**, grain-house factor (as in A) | A refusal that will hold up | Refuse without an entry to cite | Pays for entries that justify his refusals | Hands the player the forged slip | no |
| **Dorota Wilk**, veteran porter (as in A) | A route she can defend | Carry a load whose entry she knows is false | Knows which gates ask for the entry | Sells what she knows, at a price | yes |
| **Antek Raczek**, bailiff's runner | A true copy of an entry for a creditor | Take a forged one to court | Sends the player to check an entry against the porters' book | The check costs a day | no |

- Central conflict through people: the archive is the city's memory, and a trade exists in editing it.
- Crowd effect: the more players cite a forged entry, the more it is attested (A's attested-entry rule), so a lie becomes record by use.

**Why it differs.** The only pitch where the cast puts the central system's honesty at risk.

Hard references: Grzędowicz: shows through a familiar institution with one element bent (`world.2`: the archive that sells its past) and the dodge as information (`dialogue.1`, every answer about an entry). It does not take the intrigue or the secret-history shape; there is no hidden hand, only a trade.

Soft references: Miéville is not assigned to character-designer and is left out of the characters; the archive office is `institution.1`.

**Fit.** Strains the most. It cuts against D-0009 (old rules "always discoverable in an archive") because a discoverable entry may be false. It requires the game to keep the true predecessor chain server-side as authorial truth (D-0010), which the bible must record as a distinct layer from the attested one. It also puts the player in the position of someone who can alter the world, which is the nearest this cast comes to a chosen-one feeling.

**What we give up.** A stable archive: the whole of round 1's pitch A rests on it being trustworthy. Biggest unknown: whether players treat the archive as truth or as testimony once they know it can be bought.

**It unlocks.** (1) `character`: Zofia Czapla: character-designer. (2) A system spec `design/systems/attested-and-true`, a rule for how the game tells the two apart: game-systems-designer. (3) `history`: the dated chain of predecessors, protected, with a note of which entries are authorial truth: historian.

**Shared world.**
- Personal: entries the player has commissioned or checked, and the standing that comes with them.
- Party: a party can buy one entry together; all members are named in it.
- Shared: whether an entry is attested, flagged or struck.
- Late joiner: reads the archive as it stands, including entries that were struck, with the date and who flagged them.

## Recommendation

**A, Three Accounts**, with one seed taken from C: **Wincenty Zając**, the last clerk of a wound-up guild, added as the eighth seed. Reason: A makes the cast the faces of the chosen core, so every character page is also a faction page, and Wincenty gives the crowd's effect a person (the keeper whose fall the player can watch). Seam: Wincenty adds a fifth keeper to A's four, which costs one more faction page and one more licence in the system spec; his place in the wound-up-guild tradition has to be reconciled with Irena Szalas's ferry company so they are not the same remnant twice.

What I would not choose, and why:
- **B** as the cast: it is a level layout with names, and the few new people (Bronek, Stach, Wanda) can come into A later as route staff.
- **C** as the cast: sound for stories, but it moves the weight onto losers and bystanders and away from the four factions the core created. Józef Lis and Agata Rogal can become story-only characters later.
- **D**: it is a good idea for a later season, once the archive has earned trust. Now it spends the trust that A's design depends on.
