## Premise

Łódź stands on the river Żółwia, and its present rules make sense only to someone who knows which older rule each one replaced. At every gate a clerk applies the posted rule; somewhere in the city a keeper (a guild, a shrine, a remnant) still holds the rule it replaced, and some loads move only under that older rule. The recurring pressure is a load that must be delivered under a rule whose reason nobody states aloud.

The first part is one reach of the river, about an hour's walk end to end, and its two banks. The rest of the city exists as named gates on its edge that do not open yet.

Fantasy stays in the background. It shows as a price, a refusal or a form to fill in, never as an event or a spectacle.

## Player Fantasy / Reader Experience

- **In the game:** the player is one novice porter among many. The satisfaction is reading a gate that could not be read last week: a fee paid, a reading bought, a licence from one keeper that closes a door at another. Progression is access, not power. Nobody in the world treats the player as special, and the player's choice of which book to carry under helps decide which keepers stand and which fold.
- **In the stories:** one character's knowledge only, close third, past tense. A fact reaches the reader after the character has paid for it. The ending settles the practical problem (the load is delivered, refused or lost) and leaves the cost of the rule open.

## Tone Guardrails

| Element | Allowed | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Humour | dry, from procedure: a stamp of the wrong colour, a dodge | puns, jokes that need a reader to know the setting's lore |
| Dread | stated as a logistical fact, then the next step | exclamations, dramatic build-up |
| Violence | rare; told as a fact and its consequence | spectacle, gore, a fight as a set piece |
| Intimacy | favours owed, trust given to a clerk or a porter | anything sexual (PEGI 16) |
| Ambiguity | `Unknown:` and attributed accounts ("the porters' book holds...") | a narrator or a character who turns out to be right about the past |
| Fantasy | a line item: a price, a refusal, a form | a marvel described for its own sake |
| Scale | a reach, a bridge, a stair | a war, a god, a city-wide event |

No single mood is required in every scene; a scene may be funny, tense or flat, and the plainest sentence is the gravest.

## Design Pillars

| # | Pillar | Test (what a scene or page must do) | Counterexample (what breaks it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every rule has a predecessor | A rule entry gives the rule today, the rule it replaced, why it changed, and who still keeps the old one | A rule that simply exists |
| 2 | Facts are paid for | The character or player pays a fee, time or a favour before learning a reason | A clerk who explains the history unprompted |
| 3 | The past is held by people | Every account of why is attributed to someone, and where the world does not say it reads `Unknown:` | The narrator states the true reason |
| 4 | Consequences are practical | The scene ends with a load delivered, refused or lost, and the cost of the rule left open | The scene ends with a revelation of who really runs the city |
| 5 | Nobody is special | An NPC treats the player as one of many porters, on the same terms as anyone | Someone recognises the player as the one |
| 6 | The crowd decides which pasts survive | A keeper's standing traces to what players carried, and a fold is posted as a notice | A keeper falls by plot decree |
| 7 | The marvel is a line item | Anything fantastic appears as a price, a refusal or a form | A marvel described as spectacle |

Themes (pillars 1 to 4) are kept apart from physical invariants. This proposal states no physical law of the world.
